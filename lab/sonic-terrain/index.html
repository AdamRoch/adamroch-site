THE BROWSER AS AN INSTRUMENT

DRAG THE TERRAIN · IT PLAYS BACK

[ How it's built ]

The terrain is a line-mesh grid: 96 frequency bins across, 160 rows of scrolling history receding to the horizon. Each animation frame reads the AnalyserNode (2048-point FFT), maps bins on a log-ish curve so the bass band stays wide the way the ear hears it, applies attack-fast / release-slow smoothing, and pushes a new row onto the ring buffer. The newest row sits about a metre and a half from the lens, so the first dozen rows dissolve in rather than arriving: otherwise the sheet's cut edge fills the near corner as a flat plate, and this way the present comes out of the dark instead. 

// history recedes: shift everything one row back,
// then write the new frame at row 0
history.copyWithin(COLS, 0, history.length - COLS);
history.set(values, 0);

A second ring rides alongside it holding heat: the per-bin spectral flux, plus whatever you just plucked. Because the heat belongs to the row and not to the near edge, a beat is written once and then travels to the horizon as a shockwave, and a pluck leaves a scar that scrolls away. The same flux, summed across the spectrum and measured against its own slow average, lifts the exposure, swells the bloom and flares the horizon for a moment. 

// this frame's transient, then it belongs to the row
const rise = Math.max(0, target - prevRow[c]);
heatRow[c] = Math.min(1.5, rise * 4.5 + pulse[c] * 1.1);

Colour carries the frequency axis: hue is pitch, running from deep ember in the bass through amber and gold to a pale top end, and brightness is loudness, burning to a white core. Underneath, the terrain is mirrored through y = 0 into a dark floor: foreshortened, absorbed with depth, raised to a power so that only the bright ridges come back, and then faded out in screen space so the surface has a far edge. Without that last one the reflection's tallest rows are its nearest ones, so they project furthest down the frame and run off the bottom of it, and a floor with no far limit just reads as a second landscape hanging upside down.

The sound is pure WebAudio, no samples: a generative dark-ambient drone of detuned sine pairs a fifth apart through a lowpass filter wobbled by a slow LFO, a noise wash, and a slow lub-dub of sub thump and broadband click every 2.6 seconds, which is what the terrain punches on. Mic mode is analysis-only: the input is never routed to the speakers, so there's no feedback. Dragging across the terrain plucks it: raycast position maps to a minor-pentatonic triangle-oscillator note, so everything you play is in key. 

// position across the terrain → pentatonic note over ~2.5 octaves
const semis = 12 * Math.floor(steps / PENTA.length)
            + PENTA[steps % PENTA.length];

The lines render in linear HDR, bloom at half resolution, then one final pass tone maps, adds the horizon glow, fades the picture down into its own margins so the terrain ends inside the frame rather than at the edge of it, and lays down grain and dither, sized in device pixels. The exposure is set against the surface's own slow level, so a quiet phase is still quieter than a loud one and is never a frame with nothing in it.
